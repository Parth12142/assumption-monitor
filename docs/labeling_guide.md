# Labeling guide (version v1)

Project: Assumption Monitor. Read this before you label, and add new rules at the bottom.

## What we label

Every sentence gets one answer:

- **a** = assumption (then you also pick a topic)
- **n** = not an assumption
- **s** = unsure (we look at it again later)

## A sentence is an ASSUMPTION when all three are true

1. It looks forward. It says what will happen, what is expected, or what the plan takes for granted.
2. It is about something outside the company's own control: a price, a rate, demand, suppliers, laws, wages, exchange rates.
3. The plan depends on it, or it could be checked later against data.

Typical signals: "we assume", "we expect ... to remain", "is projected to", "based on the assumption that", "we anticipate prices will".

## A sentence is NOT an assumption when it is

- **History:** "Sales rose 5% last year."
- **A description:** "We operate 22 stores in Gujarat."
- **Legal boilerplate:** "Actual results may differ materially from these statements."
- **A risk or a possibility with no stated expectation:** "Fuel prices could rise." or "Rates may affect our results."
- **A plan or promise about the company's own actions:** "We will open 12 new stores."
- **An expectation about the company's own results, with no outside driver named:** "We expect our net income to increase."
- **Only a topic mention:** "Management discussed interest rates yesterday."
- **An opinion about the present:** "We believe our facilities are adequate."

## Topics (only for assumptions; pick the main one)

| Number | Topic | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | fuel | oil, gas, diesel, electricity and energy prices |
| 2 | supplier | suppliers, raw material supply, delivery, supply chain |
| 3 | demand | customers, sales volume, market growth, consumer spending |
| 4 | rates | interest rates and borrowing costs |
| 5 | currency | exchange rates |
| 6 | labor | wages, hiring, workforce, labor supply |
| 7 | regulation | laws, taxes, tariffs, rules and approvals |
| 8 | other | anything else (inflation, weather, technology, competitors) |

## Hard cases (add a line every time you meet a new one)

- "We expect interest rates to remain low, which supports our borrowing plan." -> a, rates
- "Interest rates may rise." -> n (only a possibility)
- "We expect to complete the acquisition in the third quarter." -> n (the company's own action)
- "Our budget assumes inflation of 3%." -> a, other (inflation has no topic of its own yet)
- Two topics in one sentence -> pick the first one mentioned.

## Rules for you

- Decide in under a minute. If you are still unsure, press **s**.
- Judge the sentence only. Do not think about what a model would say.
- Do not change old labels when you add a new rule. Write the rule here, and use label version v2 when you re-label.
- Aim for fewer than about 10% unsure answers.
